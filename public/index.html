How to read these scores

CMR combines opponent-adjusted performance, results, schedule strength and recent form on a 0–100 scale. Category scores are normalized within each fighter’s division. All-division rankings compare those normalized scores.

Sample strength is a 0–100 index based on bout count and cage time. It is not an accuracy probability or confidence interval. Provisional fighters have limited evidence; their profiles show performance CMR alongside the sample-adjusted ranked CMR.

Search keeps each fighter’s rank within the selected division and metric. “Data through” identifies the latest included event date. Active status is based on recent UFC activity and known retirement overrides. See model validation →

Early CageMetrix ratings are a descriptive model and will change as the methodology, source coverage and active-roster verification improve.

HOW THE MODEL THINKS

Performance is only meaningful in context.

CageMetrix starts with familiar fight statistics, but it does not reward the same number equally against every opponent. The model asks what each opponent normally produces and allows, then measures how far the fighter moved them away from that expectation.

01

Build the expectation

For each bout, CageMetrix estimates the opponent's normal striking and wrestling production and what that opponent typically allows to others in the same division.

02

Measure overperformance

Landing five significant strikes per minute means more against a fighter who normally allows three than against one who normally allows six. Defense works the same way: suppression of a dangerous opponent is rewarded.

03

Weight the evidence

Recent bouts matter more than old ones, stronger opponents carry more weight, and current-division performances are compared against current-division baselines. Competitive results and schedule strength are tracked separately from technical output.

04

Account for uncertainty

A four-fight prospect can post elite underlying numbers without instantly outranking established contenders. Small samples are shrunk toward the middle and labeled provisional until the model has enough evidence.

WHY TWO CMR NUMBERS CAN APPEAR

Performance CMR vs. Ranked CMR

Performance CMR shows the underlying output before sample penalties. Ranked CMR applies bout-count and cage-time adjustments. The blend remains a descriptive model; historical testing has not established an overall forecasting advantage over Elo.
